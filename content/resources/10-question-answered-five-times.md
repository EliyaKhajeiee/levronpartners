## Start with the one you’re tired of

Pick **one question that comes up every week**. Start with the one your team is tired of answering, not the hardest one. Write down the answer you actually give, including anything that depends on the job or the written quote.

A saved reply also gets the first response out faster. The window that matters is still the one in [the 5-minute rule](/resources/5-minute-rule-lead-response-contractors).

Then paste this into the AI tool you already use:

```text
Customers often ask us:

"[Paste the question, without the customer's personal details]"

Here is how our business actually answers it:

"[Write your rough answer. Include the conditions and what our
team needs to check before making a promise.]"

Turn this into a reply our office can save and reuse.

- Answer the question first.
- Use three sentences or fewer.
- Sound like a person at a construction or home service company.
- Keep the conditions and the next step.
- Do not invent prices, schedules, warranty terms, or what's
  included in a customer's quote.

Then write a shorter version for a text message.
```

## What that looks like on an install

Here’s an HVAC example. A homeowner asks, “Does the install quote include removing my old unit?”

The answer depends on the scope in *their* estimate. Your team’s rough note might say: “Check the written quote. If removal is listed, it’s included. If it isn’t, confirm with the tech before saying yes.”

That can become a saved reply:

> “I can check that against your written estimate. If removal is listed in the scope, it’s included; if it isn’t, I’ll confirm with the technician before giving you an answer.”

It won’t eliminate the need to check the quote. It gives everyone the same clear response while that check happens.

## Save the version you would actually send

Read the AI’s version before saving it. Fix anything you wouldn’t say or couldn’t stand behind. Put the approved reply where your office already works — a shared document, your email templates, or saved replies in your messaging tool. Review it when your process or terms change.

Then do the next question: warranty coverage, scheduling, what the homeowner needs to do after an inspection. Over time, the answers your best people give stop living only in their heads.
