The story that matters most this week is a scam, not a product.

Three things happened in AI between November 2nd and the 8th that are worth five minutes of your time if you run a trade or home service business. All three landed on Thursday.

## Thursday: Prosecutors say AI copies of CEOs stole $129 million

On Thursday, federal prosecutors in Manhattan charged three people over a scam that used AI to copy the voices, and sometimes the faces, of company CEOs. These are allegations. The defendants are presumed innocent.

Here is how prosecutors say it worked. The scammers emailed an employee, often in accounting, posing as the CEO and asking for help with a confidential financial transaction. Then they set up a phone or video call where AI mimicked the CEO's voice, and sometimes the CEO's image. The "CEO" sent the employee to a lawyer at a major law firm, who was also an impostor. The "lawyer" told the employee to wire large payments to accounts the group controlled. The money was then converted to cryptocurrency to hide where it went.

Prosecutors put the losses at more than $129 million. The Associated Press reports the group targeted thousands of companies worldwide over more than a year and a half. The victims named include hospital systems in Missouri and California, a bank in Rhode Island, a UK financial technology company, a Massachusetts mutual fund company, and a Florida pet food company.

U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald said AI "offers tremendous potential," adding that "the public should recognize that there are some who seek to use this emerging technology to pursue criminal goals."

**What that tells you:** no contractors are named in the reports. The setup is what carries over. It needed one employee who handles payments and believes a voice. For a trade business, that is the call to your office manager or bookkeeper that sounds like you and wants a payment moved today. That comparison is ours, not something the reports say.

The reports we read didn't include advice on protecting yourself. This part is ours, not the prosecutors': a voice or a face on a call is no longer proof of who you're talking to. If a request to move money arrives that way, confirm it by calling the person back on a number you already had.

## Thursday: Meta paused terms that would have let it crawl websites tied to business accounts

On Thursday, Nieman Lab reported that Meta paused planned changes to the rules for businesses that use its products. The changes were due to start on November 20th. Meta's business help center notice says: "These updates are now paused and will not take effect on November 20, 2026." The current terms stay in effect.

The paused updates covered four sets of terms: Commercial Terms, Product Catalog Terms, Self-Serve Ad Terms, and Ad Creative Generative AI Terms. The Next Web notes that the Commercial Terms apply to any business using Meta's products, including one that only runs a Facebook Page.

According to Nieman Lab, the proposed terms would have let Meta scrape the websites of its business account users. The Next Web describes it as Meta using automated software to crawl pages linked to a business and pull updates from them. It adds that legal experts told Nieman Lab that content could then be used to train Meta's AI. Meta said the update only combined crawling rules that already existed across several documents, and that site owners can still block its crawler with a small file on their website called robots.txt. The Next Web notes the terms themselves did not say that.

Meta's statement: "We're pausing the changes as we continue to engage with stakeholders and take our time to get this right." Meta has not said when the changes will come back or whether they will be rewritten.

**What that tells you:** nothing changes for you today. If your company has a Facebook Page, these terms apply to you, and the ones you already agreed to stay in effect. The thing to take from this is what Meta wanted to do: crawl pages linked to a business. It's paused, not cancelled. This is a summary of news reports, not legal advice.

## Thursday: Check your Message button on Google Maps today

Google's monthly Small Business Bulletin, posted Thursday, says messaging is now easier to find in Google Maps on Android and iPhone. If your business profile has chat, customers see a Message button next to Call on your Maps listing. Before, they had to scroll deep into your profile to find chat. Tapping the button sends them to the channels you've linked, which can include WhatsApp and text messages.

Chat on business profiles isn't new. The button sits in a new spot. Neither report describes anything you need to turn on. Search Engine Roundtable's advice is to check that the messaging channel linked to your Business Profile is the right one, so chats reach you.

**What that tells you:** check this today. Open Google Maps on your phone, look up your company, and see whether there's a Message button next to Call. If there is, find out where a tap on it goes. A homeowner comparing contractors in Maps can reach you in one tap. If the channel behind that button is a text line you check after dinner, or a WhatsApp account nobody watches, that homeowner is waiting.

## Putting it together

A scammer copied a CEO's voice. Meta paused plans to crawl pages linked to business accounts. And a Google bulletin reminded owners that customers can message you straight from Maps.

Your voice, your website, and your inbox are how homeowners reach you. This week, all three were in play.

Two of the three have something you can check today. The Meta one is paused, not gone.

P.S. Ask your office manager or bookkeeper: "If I called, sounded exactly like me, and told you to pay someone today, what would you do?" It takes ten seconds, and the answer tells you where you stand.
