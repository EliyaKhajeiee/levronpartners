Four things happened in AI between October 5th and the 11th that are worth five minutes of your time if you run a trade or home service business.

All four point the same way. AI is moving into the places you and your customers already are.

## Monday: ChatGPT is adding picture ads

OpenAI announced it will test a new kind of ad in ChatGPT: picture ads that show up while ChatGPT is making an image for someone. The test starts later in October, in the US only, with a first group of advertisers.

OpenAI says the ads will be clearly labeled and kept separate from the image being made, and that ads do not change ChatGPT's answers. Only people on the free and lower cost Go plans see them. OpenAI also added tools that help advertisers see whether an ad led to a sale.

**What that tells you:** your work sells on photos. A finished bathroom, a new roof, a clean install. Picture ads put that kind of image in front of people who are already thinking about a project. It is a test with a small group of advertisers, so there is nothing to sign up for yet. It is worth watching, because ChatGPT is being built into an ad platform piece by piece.

## Tuesday: AI that books service calls got its first rulebook

Meta and an AI customer service company called Sierra announced a shared standard for how personal AI assistants deal with businesses. They call it the Personal Agent Protocol. Walmart, Shopify, and Stripe are among the partners.

Sierra is run by Bret Taylor, who is also chairman of OpenAI. He told CNBC: "Companies will know when it's a personal agent versus an actual person." He compared it to signing into a website with your Google or Facebook account.

The first version is due by the end of October. Nothing is live yet, and no other businesses have signed on.

**What that tells you:** picture a homeowner telling their phone, "find a plumber who can come Tuesday and book it." Today, when an assistant like that hits your website, you can't tell it apart from a person or a junk bot. This standard is the first attempt to fix that: the assistant says who it works for, and the business decides what it can do. Nothing to do today. But if customers book with you online, this is the plumbing that decides whether AI assistants can book with you too.

## Tuesday: Claude moved into Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides

Anthropic released Claude for Google Workspace, in beta, for anyone on a paid Claude plan. It puts Claude in a sidebar next to the file you have open. It reads the doc, sheet, or slide deck and makes changes right in the file.

In Sheets, it can write formulas, build pivot tables and charts, and add new tabs. In Slides, it builds new slides that match the look of your deck. You can approve each edit before it lands. Claude can only open the files your Google sharing settings already let you open.

**What that tells you:** if you keep job costs, material lists, or estimates in Google Sheets, this is the part to notice. Formulas and pivot tables are where most people get stuck, and now you can ask in plain words instead. It needs a paid Claude plan, and it is still a beta. If your sheets have customer names and addresses, remember the AI will see whatever is in the file.

## Wednesday and Thursday: ChatGPT stopped answering only in text

OpenAI rolled out a new model, GPT-6, with what it calls Intelligent UI. Paying users got it Wednesday. Free users got it Thursday, worldwide.

ChatGPT answers can now include buttons you can tap, charts you can adjust, forms, and small calculators built for your question. One example OpenAI showed was a personal savings calculator. If you prefer plain text, you can turn the visuals down. OpenAI says ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion people a week.

**What that tells you:** two sides of this. For you, ask it to work out a payment plan or compare two material options, and it may hand you a working calculator instead of a paragraph. Check the math before anything goes to a customer, because a wrong number now arrives looking finished. For your customers, the same free tool is in their pocket. Some of them may start building their own rough math on what a job should cost before they ever call you.

## Putting it together

ChatGPT is turning into an ad platform. Big companies started writing the rules for AI that books with businesses. Claude moved into Google Sheets. And free ChatGPT started building tools instead of writing paragraphs.

AI stopped being a separate place you go. It is showing up inside the things you and your customers already use.

That makes it easier to start, because there is less to learn. It also means checking its work matters more, because the answers look more finished than ever.

P.S. Next time you open ChatGPT, ask it to build a quick calculator for a job you price every week. If it builds one, check one answer by hand against your own numbers before you trust the rest.
