## The roofing company that blamed traffic

A roofing company was spending $4,000 a month on Google Ads. Leads were coming in at a healthy clip. The close rate was low. The owner blamed traffic quality — figured the platform was sending junk leads.

When we mapped the actual follow-up flow, the average response time to a new inquiry was **four hours and twenty minutes.**

The traffic wasn't the problem. The window was gone before anyone called back.

## What the data actually says

Studies on lead conversion consistently put the real window at **five minutes.** After that, the odds of reaching the prospect at all drop by more than 80%.

Home service and contracting businesses aren't usually losing leads because they lack volume. They're losing them in the twenty minutes after the inquiry lands — while the homeowner is still comparing options and still willing to talk. By the four-hour mark, they've typically already booked an estimate with someone else.

## What a response layer actually looks like

The fix isn't a faster salesperson or another estimator. It's a **response layer** that fires the moment a form is submitted or a call is missed — not whenever someone gets back to their desk.

That layer does four things automatically:

- **Instant acknowledgment** — the homeowner knows their inquiry was received within 60 seconds
- **Qualification question** — one targeted question that moves the job forward (roof age, square footage, urgency)
- **Booking link** — a direct path to schedule an estimate without waiting for a callback
- **Consistent timing** — running at 9pm on a Friday exactly the same as 9am on a Tuesday, because roof leaks and burst pipes don't keep business hours

No one has to be at a desk. No one has to remember. The system responds every time, on every job type.

## Speed is a system trait

Contractors who respond fast don't have faster estimators. They have better infrastructure.

The owner replying in four hours isn't lazy — they're running a process that requires a human at every step, and that human is usually on a roof or in a crawlspace. The competitor replying in four minutes isn't working harder — they built a system that doesn't wait for them to be free.

If you're spending real money to generate leads and losing them to slow follow-up, you don't have a marketing problem. You have a response-time problem — and response-time problems are solvable with automation, not headcount.
